# Books to read aloud

These are for you to read to your child, not for your child to read. The practice texts in the app are for that. Story time is about rich language and a good story, and the books below have both.

Charlotte Mason, the English educator a lot of homeschoolers follow, called them "living books": a book "which finds its way to the mind of a young reader", written by someone who cared about the story, as opposed to "twaddle" written down to children. She didn't hurry reading lessons either - "There is no occasion to hurry the child" - which is the spirit of this app too.

A short list, on purpose. Read the same ones again and again; small children love that, and it's good for them.

## From the Charlotte Mason tradition

These come from the [AmblesideOnline list for children under six](https://www.amblesideonline.org/ao-y0-bks). The first four are old enough to be free on Project Gutenberg, so you can read them from your phone.

- **The Tale of Peter Rabbit** by Beatrix Potter (1902) - a naughty rabbit, a furious gardener and a safe return home. Short enough for a 3-year-old. [Free on Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14838).
- **The Real Mother Goose**, illustrated by Blanche Fisher Wright (1916) - nursery rhymes. Pick two or three at a time and let them join in. [Free on Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10607).
- **A Child's Garden of Verses** by Robert Louis Stevenson (1885) - poems about swings, shadows and bedtime. One poem is enough. [Free on Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25609).
- **When We Were Very Young** by A. A. Milne, illustrated by E. H. Shepard (1924) - bouncy verse about small children and their days, from the man who later wrote Winnie-the-Pooh. [Free on Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/70271).
- **Blueberries for Sal** by Robert McCloskey (1948) - a girl and a bear cub each wander off from their mothers on the same hill. The "kuplink, kuplank, kuplunk" of berries in a pail is made for reading aloud. You'll need to buy or borrow this one ([publisher's page](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/306398/blueberries-for-sal-by-robert-mccloskey/9780140501698/)).

## African and Kenyan books

Books by African writers, Kenyan and East African where I could find them. Some are hard to buy in Kenya; I've said where.

- **Sulwe** by Lupita Nyong'o, illustrated by Vashti Harrison (2019) - a dark-skinned Kenyan girl wishes she were lighter, and a story about Night and Day changes her mind. Beautiful, but it deals with colourism head on, so it suits 4- and 5-year-olds more than 3-year-olds. Text Book Centre stocks it in [English](https://textbookcentre.com/catalogue/sulwe-english-lupita-nyongo-paperback_267657/) and in [Kiswahili](https://textbookcentre.com/catalogue/sulwe-kiswahili-lupita-nyongo-paperback_297564/).
- **The Wooden Camel** by Wanuri Kahiu, illustrated by Manuela Adreani (2017) - Etabo, a Turkana boy, dreams of racing camels. Funny and tender, best for 4 and up. Kahiu is the Kenyan filmmaker behind Rafiki. Hard to find in Kenyan shops; [her site](https://wanuri-kahiu.squarespace.com/read) has buying links, or ask a bookseller to order it.
- **Chicken and Millipede** by Winny Asara, illustrated by Magriet Brink - a football rivalry between two friends ends with Chicken swallowing Millipede, and an escape that makes children shriek. Free, from African Storybook, in [English](https://www.africanstorybook.org/newviewer/index.php?bt=3&dual=false&id=9094) and in Kiswahili as [Kuku na Jongoo](https://www.storybookscanada.ca/stories/sw/0141/).
- **Baby Goes to Market** by Atinuke, illustrated by Angela Brooksbank (2017) - Mama shops at a busy Nigerian market while Baby, on her back, is quietly handed snacks - and eats some and stashes the rest. Counting, food and a joke the child sees coming. [Publisher's page](https://www.walker.co.uk/9781406365160/baby-goes-to-market/); not easy to find in Kenya.
- **And Also!** by Lauren Beukes, illustrated by Anja Venter (2017) - a little sister keeps adding to her brother's games until he lets her in. Free from [Book Dash](https://bookdash.org/books/and-also/), a South African publisher that gives its books away.
- **Handa's Surprise** by Eileen Browne - Handa carries a basket of fruit to her friend in a Luo village while animals help themselves behind her back. Children love knowing what Handa doesn't. Eileen Browne is British, and one critic argues the book shows a narrow picture of Africa, so read it alongside the others rather than on its own. [Bookstop](https://bookstop.co.ke/handa-s-surprise.html) has listed it.

## More free books

- [African Storybook](https://www.africanstorybook.org/) - a large library of short picture books from across Africa, many in Kiswahili and other African languages. Free to read, print and share.
- [Book Dash](https://bookdash.org/) - South African picture books, free to download and print.
- [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/) - older classics that are out of copyright.
